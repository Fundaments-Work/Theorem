# Highlights and Notes

Highlight text in six colors, attach notes, save bookmarks, mark up PDFs with a pen, and share any highlight as an image.

Highlights, notes and bookmarks work in books, PDFs, comics and RSS articles. Everything is stored on your device. To get highlights and notes into another app, see [Markdown export for Obsidian and Logseq](vault-sync.md).

## Highlight text

1. Open a book and select some text with the mouse or your finger.
2. In the pop-up that appears, click one of the six color swatches. You can also press the number key `1` to `6`.
3. The text stays highlighted, and Theorem saves the highlight.

The six colors carry a default label:

| Key | Color | Label |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `1` | Yellow | Important |
| `2` | Green | Key Idea |
| `3` | Blue | Interesting |
| `4` | Red | Critical |
| `5` | Orange | Action |
| `6` | Purple | Beautiful |

The same pop-up also has these buttons:

- **Copy** copies the selected text.
- **Add Note** opens a note box for the passage.
- **Define** looks the selection up in your dictionaries. See [Dictionary and vocabulary](dictionary.md).

### Change or delete a highlight

1. Click or tap an existing highlight. The same pop-up opens.
2. Choose another color swatch to recolor it, click Add Note to attach a note, or click Delete.
3. When Theorem asks "Delete this highlight and any associated notes?", confirm.

A highlight is one continuous range of text. A sentence that runs across a page or column break is still a single highlight. If you select text that overlaps or touches an existing highlight in the same chapter, Theorem merges them into one. The oldest highlight keeps its notes. A highlight never crosses from one chapter to the next, and PDF highlights are per page and are not merged.

## Add a note

1. Select text and click Add Note, or open an existing highlight and click Add Note.
2. Type in the Add your note box.
3. Click Save, or press `Ctrl+Enter`.

To change a note later, open the Annotations panel and choose Edit note, or use the Workbench. See [Workbench and bookmarks](workbench.md).

## Bookmark a page

1. Click the bookmark button in the reader toolbar, or press `Ctrl+D`.
2. To remove the bookmark, click the button or press `Ctrl+D` again on the same page.

Bookmarks mark a position. They have no text and no color. They are not written to your Markdown export.

## Review a book's annotations

1. In the reader, press `Ctrl+A`, or open the More options menu and choose Annotations & Notes.
2. Switch between the Bookmarks and Highlights lists.
3. Click an entry, or its Jump to link, to go to that spot in the book.
4. Click the three-dot menu on an entry to choose Edit note, Share or Delete.

## Mark up a PDF

PDFs have a tool palette in addition to text highlights.

1. Click the tools button near the edge of the page (Open annotation tools).
2. Choose a tool:
   - **Highlight** highlights selected text.
   - **Pen** draws freehand lines.
   - **Text** adds a note on the page. The button tooltip reads Add note.
   - **Eraser** removes marks.
3. Pick a color swatch. The highlight tool and the pen remember their own colors.
4. Click the tools button again (Close annotation tools) to leave drawing mode.

EPUB and other reflowable books support highlights and notes but not drawing.

## Share a highlight as an image

1. Open the menu of a highlight in the Annotations panel or in the Workbench and choose Share, then Share as Image.
2. In the Share Highlight window, choose the shape: Square (1:1) or Story (9:16).
3. Choose a look: App Theme, Dark, Tinted or Sepia.
4. Click Download to save a PNG. On Android the image is saved to your gallery. Where it is offered, click Share... to use your device's share sheet.

Choosing Share, then Share on X, sends text instead of an image: the quote, the book title and author, and your note if the highlight has one. Check the text before you post it.
